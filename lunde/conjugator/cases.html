Nouns, pronouns, adjectives

Cases

Case marking is tripartite: the transitive subject (A), the intransitive subject (S) and the object (O) each have their own form. Unusually, A is the bare form, and S is marked with -m.

  • Ki labi guse. I love you: ki is bare (A), guse takes -se (O).
  • Kim eruj. I die: kim takes -m (S).

Endings

Each case is a case consonant + -e (singular), -i (plural animate) or -en (plural inanimate). After a consonant, a goes in first.

  1. After a consonant, a is inserted before the ending: osilami, fasturam.
  2. Plural transitive subject: -i and -en after a consonant (osili, zenen); after a vowel, y or n goes in first (neyi, nenen).
  3. Comitative and abessive: the ū drops after u or ū (fastune, gūne, heneułe); a long last vowel adds v before the suffix. The engine reads "last" as word-final.
  4. The exmon participle drops its o before any ending (fastuox → fastuxam); consonant stems keep it (laboxam). ney uses ne- before every ending.
  5. Dissimilation never changes a case ending, and the word in front of it stays as it is: veve, keavave, fastuxūłe.
  6. Stress never moves: case endings don't count (īs-si-ū-ne).
  7. Which mutations the endings cause is still open.

Personal pronouns

The pronouns take the noun endings on an oblique stem. The engine builds the whole table from the stems; four forms are exceptions the doc lists, marked with *.

  • The g of the 2s oblique forms is optional: (g)use. Plural pronouns lengthen the stem vowel: (g)u- and gū-, da- and dā-.
  • Negation puts the subject pronoun in the abessive, with no verb affix: Kiūłe labi guse (I don't love you).

Demonstratives and place words

Demonstratives are bare roots used as adjectives, set by distance and by what can be known: ney this, nal that, gval yonder, maū something unseen whose existence can't be verified.

maū declines the same way (maūm, maūse) and takes maūne, maūłe, like gū.
