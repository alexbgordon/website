Lundiyani verb conjugator (Modern Lundian)

Lundiyani verbs never change form: tense, aspect, mood and negation are separate particles in a fixed order before the verb. Pick a verb to see its conjugation tables; every phrase is built from the rules of the Lundiyani grammar, and anything the grammar does not specify is shown as a dash.

    Open questions

    What the grammar leaves unspecified. Until a question is decided the conjugator follows the slot order and marks the result.

    1. Where does lan go next to an aspect particle? The grammar puts it after kiena and tefri (ale kiena lan lo eden); with talei and mai the conjugator follows the slot table (aspect, then lan), which no example shows.
    2. Does i tefri lo lai eden mean "had eaten" or "had been eating"? How is the other one said? (open in the grammar)
    3. Is the perfect always tefri lo? The only examples have lo; tefri with na, lei or no tense is built by the slot order but not confirmed.
    4. Are talei (habitual) and mai (just now) used without lo, for a present habit or with the future? The examples only have talei lo and mai lo.
    5. Does kiena lan combine with na or lei, or with no tense ("not yet" now)? Only kiena lan lo is shown.
    6. Can lai (progressive) and dei (passive) combine, and in which order? The slot table gives them one slot, so the conjugator offers one or the other.
    7. Commands: is the person after the verb also used for i, ne, ole and nilie? The grammar shows ov lua (o), ilie, a and ale, and only lan after ov.
    8. ta (to have): can it take lai or dei, or aspect particles? Only ta i, ta i lo, ta i lan are shown.
