Numbers

Numbers

Lundiyani counts in base twelve, as Lunde does. The rules are the same; the words have changed in pronunciation.

  • Zero is ułe. An empty place inside a bigger number is simply left out: iyion-na (145).
  • é- is used only when counting (é-na, é-re); before a noun the bare form is used.
  • Multiply: the multiplier goes first, joined (resion, 24). Add: the smaller number follows after a dash (sion-na, 13). Biggest power first.
  • After any number above one, the noun takes the plural.
  • Ordinals: the number + -de (-ade after a consonant), as in Lunde: na-de (first), sion-ade (twelfth). Assumed; see the open questions.

Still open

Open questions

  • The counting prefix: the Lundiyani grammar gives é- (é-na). Every other number has lost its accent, so it may now be e-. Kept as é-.
  • The ordinal ending is assumed unchanged from Lunde (number + -de / -ade); the Lundiyani grammar does not mention ordinals.
  • Whether ułe has a counting form (é-ułe?) and an ordinal. Not shown.
  • Numbers above 248,831: no power above ayation (12⁴) is named yet.
