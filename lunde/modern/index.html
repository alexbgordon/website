Lundiyani grammar (Modern Lundian)

Lundiyani is the modern form of Lunde (Old Lundian): an isolating lingua franca spoken across the conworld, much later than Lunde and very different from it. Many peoples speak it, so it has dialect and register doublets, borrowings, and changes from Lunde that aren't always regular; the imperfection is intended. Verbs never change form, and the only grammatical suffixes are possessive -de and the plurals -i, -en and -nen. Most words have no worked-out etymology, and none is needed.

Phonology

Sounds

Lundiyani has lost the Lunde h, keeps x as its own sound, and writes ł and ğ as Lunde does.

  • There is no h sound: Lunde h is lost (Lunde hara → ara, three). x is a different sound and stays.
  • au and aw spell the same sound.
  • Final a → ai happened in some words only; it is not a rule.

Spelling

  • ł is one sound, the Welsh ll.
  • A written xl is x + l, where a word in x meets a suffix in l: kaxli (kaxi + -li).
  • ğ replaces gy.

Syntax

Word order

The basic order is subject, verb, object, and modifiers come before what they modify.

  1. Before the noun: articles, demonstratives, possessors and adjectives. nei kawe on (this tall person), ide kora (my cat).
  2. Before the verb: negation and every tense, aspect or mood particle.
  3. After the noun: relative clauses. i on xaon lo lai yada (the person who was singing).
  4. At the end: the question particle me.
  5. Prepositions are separate words. No word attaches to another except the suffix -de and the plural endings.
  6. Exceptions: ta (to have) and commands put the verb before the subject.

  • lai is also the copula (i lo lai kili, I was young) and, at the start of a sentence, means “there is”: lai anei nai ci louba (there is water in the bottle); negated, lan lai anei (there’s no water).
  • lan comes after kiena and tefri, which work like adverbs: ale kiena lan lo eden (they haven’t eaten yet).
  • A modal or second verb takes the bare verb: i lan sa valia (I can’t sleep), ilie mail kapa (we need to talk).

Passive

Tense + dei + verb, with amb for the agent: ci pay lo dei raxavi amb ni wakti (the village was destroyed by the warriors); i lo dei fastixi (I was blinded). dei comes from Lunde daze (by him or her), the agent in the Lunde passive of be + participle + ablative.

ta (to have)

ta is the one verb that comes before its subject; particles and the object follow in the normal order.

Commands

Commands are ov + verb + person.

CommandMeaning

  • The person comes after the verb. With singular you it has fallen out of use (ov eden, eat!), but formal writing keeps it (ov eden o).
  • Negation follows ov: ov lan lua ilie (let’s not go).
  • ov keeps commands apart from o (you).

  • Each other: on-a-oni. ale lau on-a-oni (they love each other).
  • Reflexive: amon. a lo kanxi amon (she looked at herself).

Nouns, articles, adjectives

Nouns and adjectives

Plural nouns take -i, and the articles come from Lunde.

  • Plural -i: osil → osili (eyes), kiwuru → kiwuri (dogs), kora → kori (cats), on → oni (people).
  • Inanimate nouns take -en after a consonant and -nen after a vowel: skin → skinen (books), louba → loubanen (bottles). Irregular: yami → yamo (feet).
  • After any number above one the noun is plural: re kiwuri (two dogs).

Articles

The Lunde set without its accents. Many speakers also use i with inanimate nouns.

Adjectives

  • After lai, -de marks a permanent quality such as color or size, and only in a plain “X is ADJ” sentence: i onde osili lai lasode (the person’s eyes are blue). It never appears before a noun (ko kiwuru, a big dog) or in comparisons.
  • Without -de the word names a state or an impression: i lai kuali, literally “I am happiness” (kuali = happy, happiness).
  • Comparison: kaw + adjective + da. i wuru lai kaw ko da i kiwuru (the wolf is bigger than the dog).
  • Superlative: i kaw + adjective. a lai i kaw leia on nai ci payi’an (she is the most beautiful person in the city).

Numbers

Numbers

Lundiyani counts in base twelve, as Lunde does, with the Lunde rules.

Syntax

Questions

Yes/no questions end in me; question words stay where the answer would go, or move to the front.

  • o lo vera ne me? (Did you do it?)
  • o lai vera xa? or xa o lai vera? (What are you doing?) In place is more grammatical; both are common.
  • xaon lau o? (Who loves you?) A subject question word is already in place at the start.
  • Questions with a question word take no me.

Syntax

Clauses

Subordinate clauses open with a conjunction; relative clauses use sa after the noun.

  • and: a before a consonant, ad before a vowel. ide kora ad ode kiwuru (my cat and your dog).
  • when: xanarya after the main clause: a lo lai yada xanarya o lo luran (she was singing when you came). When the when-clause comes first, xar, though xanarya also works: xar i lo lai kili, i talei lo yada (when I was young, I used to sing).
  • if: as, with lei in both clauses. as o lei luran, i lei lai kuali (if you came, I would be happy).
  • Other conjunctions: ema (because), ant (but), anta (before), nes (until), dal or ana (while), no (or).
  • Relative clauses: noun + sa + clause, as in Lunde, for people and things: i on sa yada lai ide bana (the person who sings is my brother). ci skin sa o lo sila lai leia (the book that you wrote is beautiful). xaon is also used for people: i on xaon lo lai yada lai kuali (the person who was singing is happy).
  • That-clauses: xa. i so xa o na luran (I know that you will come).
